I was born in Berdsk, a small town in Siberia, and I always knew the big world was calling me. I just didn't know yet how loud I'd have to answer.

8 years of music school
Berdsk, Siberia

It started in the snow.

I wasn't a diligent child. My mother signed me up for ballroom dancing, gymnastics and music school (eight years of it, I counted), and I tested her patience at every one. I was quick enough to get B's without trying, and B's meant more time with friends. That seemed like a great deal.

B's were enough ✓

At twelve I started going to olympiads and found out I was not as clever as I thought. At fourteen I got into SESC NSU, a boarding school for olympiad kids, and life showed me my own mediocrity up close. Everyone around me knew what they wanted. I kept asking myself: and what about me?

age 14 · olympiad kids
SUNC NSU · Novosibirsk

A career in IT, before my first line of code.

I was good at chemistry, so everyone expected a chemist or a doctor. It didn't pull me. In my last school year I chose IT without having written a single line of code. One summer of intensive Java, a year of Python, excellent state exams, and I was in: Saint Petersburg State University.

print("hello, world")
Saint Petersburg

A dream city that wasn't mine.

Petersburg had been my dream for years, and it turned out not to be my city. The grey pressed on me, and so did the feeling that the space around me was getting smaller. I remembered the little girl who wanted to see the world, and I started planning.

grey, grey, grey…

I researched universities in every country I could think of and saved money quietly: I wasn't ready to find out how Slavic parents react to “I'm dropping out” without a plan B. The choice fell on Italy and Artificial Intelligence, the first subject that ever truly caught me. Exams, a visa, two suitcases. I left my old life and went to meet a better one.

plan B: saved quietly ✓
Lombardy · 2023

Then, the light changed.

Italy stunned me almost at once: the beauty, the opportunities, and a way of seeing the world so different from mine. I closed my first year with top marks and realised I could do much more.

first year: top marks

I didn't believe I could do this.

So I kept checking. With my friends I started entering hackathons. The first one, SYNLab in Milan, we won with ReViva, an app for stroke rehabilitation with real-time feedback.

8 hackathons and counting

Then third place in Budapest, first in Italy and third in the world in Madrid. I wanted to know whether ReViva could become a company that really helps people: an entrepreneurship course, a mentorship with EIT Health, a bootcamp at Maverx, and a win in the biomedical category at Startup Day in Bologna. After that, many more hackathons, and a shelf of trophies for a girl who used to be happy with B's.

ReViva · AI for post-stroke rehabilitation

The problem

After a stroke, recovery depends on doing the right exercises, many times, correctly. Between sessions with a therapist, nobody is there to say whether a movement was right.

What we built

An app that watches rehabilitation exercises through a camera in real time and gives immediate feedback on every movement.

My part

I led a team from biology, AI, law and management, and built the machine-learning side: skeletal keypoints from the video stream, joint angles, features for pose recognition, the logic that scores each exercise, model testing and the integration into the app.

Result

1st at SYNLab, 3rd at EIT Health i-Days, 1st in Italy and 3rd internationally at the Sopra Steria Student Challenge, winner of the biomedical category at Startup Day, and the Longevity Award at Lifebility 2026.

A meeting agent that remembers

The problem

Teams keep making decisions their company has already ruled out: a legal blocker nobody remembered, an earlier agreement, a team that's already at capacity. Usually someone notices weeks later.

What we built

A B2B meeting agent that transcribes the conversation live and checks each decision against the company's memory, showing evidence-backed warnings while people are still talking.

How it works

Real-time transcription with Speechmatics, reasoning with Gemini 2.5 Flash, and retrieval over a graph of company knowledge, all running on Vultr infrastructure that survived some questionable load at 3 am.

Result

The prize for the Best Use of Gemini 2.5 Flash from Google and 2nd place for the Best Use of Vultr, at an event by lablab.ai during AI WEEK.

Product-query NLP service

The problem

People type grocery searches like “lactose free milk 2% for pancakes”. To show the right product, a shop has to understand the pieces (type, brand, volume, percent, attributes) despite typos, brand collisions and long-tail vocabulary, at production speed.

What we built

A production-style NLP service that splits a messy query into clean entities and links them to real SKUs in the catalogue.

How it works

A multilingual BERT fine-tuned for token classification; training data enriched with OpenFoodFacts and a parsed live catalogue; a lightweight post-processor that fixes tags and returns exact character spans for the API.

Result

13th of 61 teams by macro-F1, after late-night error hunts, ruthless evals and a team split across two countries.

Intent-aware retrieval for financial QA

The problem

Someone asking “what is a bond?” needs a different answer from someone asking “should I buy bonds now?”. Standard search ranks both in the same way.

What we built

A two-stage retrieval system over 57,000+ financial documents: dense retrieval with E5 and FAISS, then cross-encoder reranking.

My part

Classifying 648 queries into 6 intent types and building intent-aware reranking for definition, explanation and advice-seeking questions. We compared BM25, dense retrieval, hybrid search, HyDE query expansion and cross-encoder fine-tuning.

Result

+1.6% P@1 and +0.5% nDCG@10 over a strong dense retrieval + cross-encoder baseline.
